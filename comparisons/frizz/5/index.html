    • Lipid/cuticle-mimicking complex in B: Phospholipids (#10), Glycolipids (#12), Glycine Soja Sterols (#13) plus a cluster of emollient oils (Hippophae Rhamnoides #6, Glycine Soja #11, Argania Spinosa #14, Linum Usitatissimum #15) — sterols and phospholipids are precisely the lipid fractions lost from a raised, frizzing cuticle; replacing them improves smoothness and moisture retention.
    • Milder surfactant system in B: SCS buffered by Cocamidopropyl Betaine (#4) plus Lauryl Glucoside (#3) is a gentler cleansing blend than A's SCS + Coco-Glucoside, so less cuticle-raising from wash-off alkalinity/surfactant stripping.
    • pH positioning: both use organic acids (A: lactic acid; B: citric acid) which favour a low pH and a contracted cuticle — a point for both, not a differentiator.
    • Penalties in A: Alcohol Denat (#18) is explicitly a drying alcohol risk for raised cuticles (position suggests it may be near or below 1%, but it sits above several other components, so it is not negligible); Menthol (#21) adds a defatting/tingling astringency with no frizz benefit. A's glycerin at #3 is comparatively high, and high glycerin load in a rinse-off can pull water into the shaft in humid conditions and promote swelling — a modest, concentration-dependent negative.
    • Compensation in A (why not confidence 5): A carries a strong protein film-forming suite (Hydrolyzed Corn/Wheat/Soy Proteins, #9/12/13), Inulin (#8) and Sodium PCA (#10), which do offer some anti-frizz film and moisture. However, without any cationic agent to anchor deposition in an anionic sulfate wash, much of this rinses away with the lather.
